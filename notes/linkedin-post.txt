I ran a language model on a €5 microcontroller. With no extra RAM. And trained it to be a dystopian sustainability cop. 🤖♻️

For a post-apocalyptic themed party, I wanted a robot that "enforces" the new regime's extremely strict sustainability rules. So I put an AI on an ESP32: a tiny chip with about 300 KB of free memory.

What it does:
🔸 Interrogates guests about their habits
🔸 Reacts to their confessions ("i took a long hot shower" → a sentence of cold showers)
🔸 Hands out absurd penalties, like composting your own shoes or apologising to the nearest plant
🔸 Generates about 21 words per second, fully offline

How it works:
✅ Built on Andrej Karpathy's llama2.c and DaveBben's ESP32 port
✅ Reads the model weights straight from flash instead of RAM, so it runs on a plain ESP32 without PSRAM
✅ Trained a custom 260K-parameter model from scratch on my laptop's CPU, on 60,000 generated interrogations, in about an hour
✅ Found and fixed bugs along the way, including one where every boot told the exact same story

The lesson: you don't always need a huge model. A tiny one with a clear, narrow job can be surprisingly fun, and it runs on hardware that costs less than a coffee.

Everything is open source, including how to train your own character:
👉 https://github.com/Dundir-Been-there-Dun-Dir/esp32-llm/tree/esp32-wroom
