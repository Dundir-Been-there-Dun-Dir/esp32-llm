SUSTAINABILITY OFFICER ROBOT - HARDWARE NOTES
=============================================

1. WHICH ESP32 FOR THE ROBOT?
-----------------------------

Recommendation: an ESP32-S3 with PSRAM (look for "R8" / "8MB PSRAM" in the name).

Why:
- 8 MB PSRAM fits a model several times bigger than the current 260K one,
  which is the biggest upgrade for a smarter, more varied officer.
  (Speed drops roughly in proportion to model size.)
- The 128-token limit can go back to 512, so the officer can remember
  earlier answers in an interrogation.
- The S3 has extra maths instructions that speed up the model.
- The original project (main branch) was written for the S3; everything we
  built moves over by switching the build target back.

Boards:

  M5Stack CoreS3
    Easiest. ESP32-S3 + 8 MB PSRAM in a finished case with touchscreen,
    speaker, microphones, camera and battery. Could be the robot's head.

  ESP32-S3-DevKitC-1 N16R8 (16 MB flash, 8 MB PSRAM)
    Most flexible and cheap (around $15). You wire up screen, speaker and
    servos yourself.

  Seeed XIAO ESP32S3 Sense
    Tiny, 8 MB PSRAM, microphone and camera built in. Good for a small robot.

Watch out: many S3 boards have NO PSRAM. That would leave you where the
WROOM-32 is now.

How guests talk to it at a party:
- Phone over Wi-Fi: the ESP32 hosts a small web page, guests type answers
  on their phones. Most practical, works on any ESP32 (including the
  current WROOM-32).
- USB keyboard: the S3 can act as a USB host, so a keyboard can plug
  straight into the robot.
- Voice: see section 2. Full speech recognition and natural speech output
  are beyond an ESP32.
- Output: show the text on a screen. For sound, a cheap I2S amplifier
  (MAX98357A) + small speaker can play beeps or pre-recorded "WARNING" clips.


2. LISTENING WITH A MICROPHONE
-----------------------------

Full speech-to-text is not possible on an ESP32, but recognising a fixed
list of words/short phrases is, using Espressif's speech library ESP-SR:

- WakeNet: listens all the time for a wake word (e.g. "Hi ESP").
  Could start a new interrogation.
- MultiNet: recognises a list of command phrases you choose, e.g.
  "I drove", "I walked", "hot shower", "burger", "plastic bag",
  "recycle", "I refuse". The English version supports up to a few hundred
  phrases (as far as I know - check the current ESP-SR docs).

Why it fits: the officer was trained on fixed confessions anyway
("i drove here", "i took a long shower", "i used a plastic bag", ...).
Each recognised phrase becomes the "CITIZEN:" line and the officer reacts
the way it was trained to. Anything not recognised can be treated as
dodging the question ("Silence is suspicious, citizen.").
Guests will soon learn what makes the robot angry - half the fun.

The catch: from what I know, ESP-SR's English command recognition runs on
the ESP32-S3 and needs PSRAM for its speech models. So it most likely won't
work on the WROOM-32 - check the current ESP-SR docs before buying. An S3
with 8 MB PSRAM could hold the speech models and a bigger officer model at
the same time.

What to get:
- All-in-one: M5Stack CoreS3 (mics, speaker, screen, S3 + PSRAM, no wiring)
- DIY: ESP32-S3 N16R8 board + INMP441 I2S microphone (a few dollars),
  optionally MAX98357A amplifier + small speaker for sound effects
- Small: XIAO ESP32S3 Sense (microphone built in)

Flow: robot asks a question (screen or speaker) -> guest says something ->
board matches it to one of the trained phrases -> officer gives its
verdict and asks the next question.

Next step once a board is chosen: set up ESP-SR with a phrase list that
matches the training data in training/generate_data.py.
